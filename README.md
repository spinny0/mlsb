# MLSB 0.0.7

**Making Liquid Sims Better** is a native Windows liquid sandbox. The Live Pouring Station starts paused with water in the cup. Open **Live → Tip**, then drag the cup sideways to start the simulation and pour into the tank. Use **Grab** to move the cup, spoon, or force field. **Space** pauses and resumes, **Step** advances one tick, and **Reset** restores the setup.

- The Layout workspace follows the Blender 5.0.1 reference. The Outliner and Properties are on the right, and the Timeline sits below the viewport. Drag panel dividers to resize them. Use **View → Split Area** for a second Physics or Shading editor, and **Ctrl+Space** to maximize the viewport.
- **F3** searches available operators. **F9** adjusts the last Object Mode move, rotation, or scale. **Shift+right-click** places the 3D cursor on the grid for object placement.
- Assign **Domain**, **Flow**, or **Effector** under **Properties → Physics → Fluid**. The domain is the selected object's axis-aligned bounds, constrained by the solver's 4 × 2.8 × 2.8 m grid. Geometry flows fill closed meshes on Reset. Inflows and outflows run during playback. An effector collides with the liquid.
- Use **Secondary Particles** under Physics to tune spray, foam, and bubbles while playing. Turbulent water produces spray and foam. High viscosity suppresses aeration in honey and syrup. Foam shades the reconstructed liquid surface, and spray and bubbles use small translucent highlights. The effects leave the liquid volume unchanged.
- Add a force field with **Shift+A**. In **Properties → Physics → Force Field**, choose Directional, Vortex, or Radial and adjust strength and radius while the scene runs. The pouring station's vortex starts at zero strength.
- **Live → Cross Section** clips the viewport along a chosen axis. Change the slice position or flip the visible side to see the interior of the tank. The liquid continues to interact with the complete scene.
- Choose **Honey on a spoon**, **Glass and wax**, or **Oil over water** to try surface physics, dripping, and two liquids. Start playback with **Space**. The spoon moves automatically during the demo.
- In **Properties → Physics**, set the starting volume in liters and select **Apply fill and reset**. Set gravity, schedule the dam gate to open on a frame, and set how long it takes to lift. The readout tracks seeded, emitted, drained, and current liters.
- Select an emitter or drain in the Outliner to set its start frame, optional stop frame, flow rate, ramp, and total volume. Emitters can build and release drops or add a separate, lighter oil phase. Frame 90 is one second. Volume limits are quantized to whole particles.
- Use **Shift+A** to add meshes, emitters, drains, glass tanks, sun lights, or cameras. Select an object in the viewport or Outliner and use **Shift+D** to duplicate it. Rotate a sun to aim it and use **Use as key light** to activate it. **View from camera** moves the viewport to a saved camera object.
- Orbit with middle mouse, pan with **Shift+middle mouse**, and zoom with the wheel. **1**, **3**, **7** select the front, right, and top views. **5** switches projection, **.** frames the selection, and **Home** frames the scene.
- **G**, **R**, and **S** move, rotate, and scale. Press **X**, **Y**, or **Z** for an axis and type a value for precision. **Enter** commits and **Esc** cancels. **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Shift+Z** undo and redo scene edits.
- Select a collider and press **Tab** to edit its mesh. **1**, **2**, and **3** select vertices, edges, and faces. Shift-click extends a selection. **G/R/S** transform the selected elements. Press **G** twice on selected vertices or edges to slide along neighboring edges. Choose grid, vertex, edge, or face snapping in the Object tab and enable **Snap** in the toolbar.
- Press **E** with faces selected, then move the mouse or type a distance to extrude. Use **X**, **Y**, or **Z** to constrain the direction. **Ctrl+R** previews a loop cut as you hover a quad edge. Scroll to choose up to eight cuts, click to start sliding, then click to place them or right-click to center them. **Ctrl+B** bevels selected separate edges shared by two angled faces. Move the mouse to adjust the width and scroll to change the segment count.
- Press **K** and click two edges of one face to make a Knife cut. For **Bisect**, drag a cut line in the viewport or use the local plane controls. You can keep both sides, or remove one side and optionally cap the cut.
- Edited meshes participate in collision and ray tracing. **Collision preview** in Edit Mode shows open edges in red and draws the shell thickness around open surfaces. The scene allows up to 16 objects and 4,096 mesh triangles.
- In the collider's **Surface physics** panel, adjust **Wetting** to change how strongly liquid clings and drags along the surface. **Animate collider** sets a start frame, end frame, travel, and tilt for moving spoons, cups, and other obstacles.
- **T** toggles the gizmo toolbar, **N** shows exact transforms, **Z** opens viewport shading, and **Ctrl+Space** maximizes the viewport. Enable **Snap** in the toolbar or hold **Ctrl** during a transform.
- **Step** advances one 1/90-second physics tick. The Timeline keeps up to 144 recent samples in memory during live playback. Scrubbing pauses playback, and **Live** returns to the latest state. The File menu saves and opens JSON scene setups, which restart their simulation on load.
- Choose water, oil, syrup, or honey and tune viscosity, cohesion, surface tension, tint, absorption, and refraction. Wetting acts within a short distance of a collider. The secondary oil has a fixed lower density and stays a separate material. Surface films and tiny droplets are limited by the solver and viewport resolution.
- **Surface Preview** traces a continuous 3D liquid surface through glass. **Ray Traced** adds scene reflections, light absorption through water, solid-object shadows, and focused light on the tank bottom and stage. In **Lighting**, enable **Refraction checkerboard** to see how the liquid bends the background. **Solid** helps inspect the liquid shape and **Particles** shows the simulation points by speed.
- In **Shading → Environment**, choose the built-in Garden panorama, the studio, or a local HDR or EXR file. Custom panoramas must be 2:1 and can be up to 8192 × 4096, with viewport textures up to 4096 pixels wide. The embedded Garden is a 2048 × 1024 HDR copy of the supplied 8K image. Rotation and exposure affect reflections and lighting. You can hide the panorama in the background.
- **Render Detail** changes viewport resolution and ray step size. **Adaptive viewport while playing** reduces render detail if frames take longer to complete. **Physics** controls pressure iterations and the emission budget.

## Build

Install Rust and the Windows C++ Build Tools with a Windows SDK, then run `./Build.ps1` in PowerShell. All runtime assets are embedded in the executable.

The CPU solver uses staggered-grid pressure projection and affine particle-to-grid transfers. It reuses the pressure solution between steps and uses a volume correction for overfilled cells. The GPU traces the reconstructed density boundary and scene geometry, with finite ray depth, Fresnel reflection, total internal reflection, and distance-based absorption. Caustics use a finite-resolution receiver map. [APIC paper](https://mass.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/JSSTS15.pdf)
